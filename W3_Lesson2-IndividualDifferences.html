Start with the people, not the average

Same task. Very different learning paths.

Think back to the stick-balancing task. Even when everyone practiced the same skill, the individual performance curves were not alike. Some people started higher. Some improved faster. Some changed very little.

The question for this lesson

Why is one person “better” at a motor task than another? And why does one person appear to improve faster? The differential approach treats those person-to-person differences as the phenomenon to explain.

A different research question

The differential approach to motor learning

The differential approach seeks to understand differences between and among people on measures of motor performance.

Experimental approach

What happens to the groups?

  • Compare practice conditions or interventions.
  • Average performance across people.
  • Use retention or transfer tests to infer learning.
Differential approach

How are the people different?

  • Keep person-to-person differences visible.
  • Use correlation to compare performance across tasks.
  • Ask: if you are good at Skill A, are you also good at Skill B?

The underlying traits

Ability and motor ability

In this course, an ability is a general trait or capacity of a person. A motor ability is an ability specifically related to performing a motor skill.

Ability

A relatively enduring characteristic

Largely innate and not modifiable by practice.

Helps determine a person’s achievement potential for specific skills.

Motor ability

An ability tied to motor performance

Each person has a variety of motor abilities.

Different motor skills depend on different combinations of these abilities.

Terminology alert

Ability is not the same thing as skill

In everyday language, we often use skill and ability interchangeably. In motor learning, they refer to very different things.

AbilitySkill
Where it comes fromInnate / relatively fixedLearned
PracticeNot modifiable by practiceModifiable with practice

Keep the distinction sharp

Dribbling a basketball is a skill. Leg-to-trunk proportions are an ability-related characteristic. One can be practiced; the other is part of the performer you bring to the task.

Defining individual differences

Two people can receive the same practice, the same instruction, and have the same motivation — yet still reach different levels of performance.

Why? Each motor skill requires a particular set of motor abilities. Those abilities help establish the performer’s achievement potential for that skill.

  • =Same amount of practice
  • =Same level and amount of instruction
  • =Same motivation
  • ≠Not necessarily the same performance outcome, because the performers may bring different motor abilities to the task.
Two competing ideas

Is there one general motor ability — or many specific ones?

General motor ability hypothesis

Many motor abilities are highly related and can be grouped into a singular, global motor ability.

Prediction: if you are good at one motor skill, you should also tend to be good at another, even an unrelated one.

Specificity of motor ability hypothesis

Many motor abilities are relatively independent. Different tasks depend on different sets of abilities.

Prediction: there should be relatively little relation between performance on different motor skills.

General motor ability: Brace (1927), McCloy (1934). Specificity of motor ability: Henry (1958/1968).

Balance as a motor ability

“Balance” may not be one thing

Balance refers to postural stability: the ability to maintain your center of gravity within the base of support. Recent research has examined whether balance is one general ability or whether it contains distinct components.

Static balance

The body is still

Dynamic balance

The body is moving

Evidence for specificity

The lecture evidence suggests that at least two separate abilities are involved in balance: static balance and dynamic balance. That means being strong on one balance task does not automatically imply being strong on every other kind of balance task.

Duquette, A. M., Senia, M., van Wyk, P. M., Baker, J., Paquin, K. C., & Horton, S. (2022). Re-examining the relationship between measures of static and dynamic balance among young adults. Current Issues in Sport Science, 8(1).

What the differential approach adds

Individual differences are part of motor learning

Group averages are useful, but they can hide the fact that learners bring different motor abilities to a task. The differential approach asks what those differences are and whether performance relationships across tasks reveal common or specific underlying abilities.

Lab connection

Balance provides a clean way to test the specificity idea: does performance on one balance task actually predict performance on another?
